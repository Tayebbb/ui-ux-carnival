01 — WELCOME 
Dear participants 
This rulebook is essential for ensuring a fair and successful experience for all. By thoroughly
reading and understanding these guidelines, you will help maintain the event's integrity and
ensure equal opportunities for all. Please take the time to review this document carefully.
Carnival 8.0's UI/UX competition runs on a structured product-design pipeline. Every team
works through six phases — understand, research, plan, design, tidy up, and hand off —
inside a single 6-hour window. 
WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY 
The goal is to make sure every participant practices how professional UI/UX work
actually happens, not just how to make one screen look polished. 
Each phase has its own time limit and its own required output, so how you work
is judged alongside the final design.

05 — PHASES 1 & 2 
Understand & research 
1 Understand the Problem 
00:00 – 00:40 · 40 MIN 
Turn the released theme into a clear problem statement before you touch a single screen. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
A one-page brief: the problem, who it is for, the goals, and the limits
3–5 user stories (“As a [user], I want [goal], so that [benefit]”), each with 1–2 ways to
tell it is done
1–2 rough user profiles — you will check these in Phase 2, so they need not be final 
Tools:  Optional. ChatGPT, Claude, or a plain notebook all work equally well here. 
Tip.  This step is graded on its own. A vague brief costs you time in every phase after it. 
2 Research Your Users 
00:40 – 01:20 · 40 MIN 
Do your research using the scenario released with the theme. There are no real users to
interview in a timed contest, so the scenario stands in for them. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
A short write-up of what you learned from the scenario — done however you like
An updated user profile — change your Phase 1 guess based on what the
scenario shows
3–5 main problems or insights you found in it 
How this works.  A written scenario is released at the start of Phase 2. Work through it
yourselves, with or without AI, inside the 40 minutes. 
Tip.  Treat it as real information, not filler — judges will check that your later decisions
come from it.

===== PAGE 6 =====

06 — PHASES 3 & 4 
Plan & design 
3 Plan the Structure 
01:20 – 02:10 · 50 MIN 
Structure the product before you touch visual design. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
A sitemap or flow diagram of the app's main sections
Rough wireframes (photos of hand sketches are fine) for the 3–5 main screens in your
user stories 
Tools:  Optional — Relume, Google Stitch, Figma low-fi frames, or pen and paper. 
Tip.  Judges want wireframes that clearly match a user story, not pretty ones. 
4 Design the Screens 
02:10 – 03:40 · 90 MIN 
Turn your structure into a real, full-colour flow. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
Finished-looking screens for the whole main journey from Phases 1–3, not just a
home screen
A short note: how does each screen serve a user story or fix a problem from Phase 2? 
Tools:  Optional — Figma, Adobe XD, Flowstep AI, or any design tool you're comfortable
with. 
Tip.  This is the single most heavily weighted phase. Protect this time block; don't let
Phase 1–3 run long into it.

===== PAGE 7 =====

07 — PHASES 5 & 6 
Tidy up & hand off 
5 Build a Clean Design File 
03:40 – 04:50 · 70 MIN 
Make your file tidy enough for a developer to pick up. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
A final design file that uses auto-layout so it resizes cleanly
A written-down colour palette and font set, with a short reason for each choice
Real text in every screen — no lorem ipsum — and the theme's logo used
Basic accessibility notes: colour contrast, alt text, and any other accessibility choices
you made 
Tools:  Optional — Figma plus plugins, or Adobe XD. 
Tip.  This is what separates a student file from a portfolio-ready one. Make your existing
screens consistent instead of adding new ones. 
6 Prototype & Handoff 
04:50 – 05:40 · 50 MIN 
Package your work the way a designer hands it to a developer or a client. 
REQUIRED DELIVERABLE 
A clickable prototype link (Figma prototype, Claude Artifacts, or similar)
A one-page handoff note: main flows, open questions, and what a developer needs
to know 
Tip.  A prototype that does not click through end to end counts as incomplete, even if the
screens look great. 
05:40 – 06:00 · Time to submit — 20 min. Late uploads are not accepted.

08 — TOOLS POLICY 
AI is optional, your thinking is 
not 
AI tools are optional at every phase, including Phase 2. Teams are free to complete the
entire sprint with traditional tools — pen and paper, Figma alone, manual research notes —
if they prefer.
The Phase 2 scenario stands in for the real users you would normally talk to. How you work
through it is up to you. 
If you use AI, we still expect to see your own thinking. Your prompts, edits,
and decisions are what get judged — not the raw output of a tool. 
SUGGESTED, NEVER REQUIRED 
Phase 1 ChatGPT, Claude, or a plain notebook 
Phase 2 Any AI, or your own notes 
Phase 3 Relume, Google Stitch, Figma low-fi, pen and paper 
Phase 4 Figma, Adobe XD, Flowstep AI 
Phase 5 Figma plus plugins, or Adobe XD 
Phase 6 Figma prototyping, Claude Artifacts

10 — JUDGING RUBRIC 
How you are scored 
Brief & User Stories 
Is the problem clear? Are the user stories specific and checkable?   
10% 
Research & User Profile 
Did you really use the scenario? Did your user profile and problem list change
from Phase 1 to 2? 
15% 
Structure & Wireframes 
Do the sitemap and wireframes clearly match the user stories?   
15% 
Screen Design 
How good the screens look, and whether the whole journey is covered — not
one hero screen. 
25% 
Design File Quality 
Auto-layout, consistent colours and fonts, real text, no placeholders.   
15% 
Prototype & Handoff 
Does the prototype click through end to end? Can someone else use your
handoff note? 
10% 
Accessibility & Usability 
Contrast, alt text, and other accessibility choices are written down, not just
claimed. 
10%
